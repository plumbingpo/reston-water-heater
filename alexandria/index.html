How Alexandria Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Alexandria Homeowners Face

Around Alexandria, water heater trouble tends to come from a handful of sources. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the rumbling and popping you may hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water should get annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater often needs code items it never had: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these is fixable, and most cost far less when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (703) 202-2745 and we'll take a look.
