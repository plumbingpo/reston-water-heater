A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Reston, VA, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Reston, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Reston Water Heater can take over from there.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. And many repairs, including gas valve and heater replacement, require a permit and inspection in most areas. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. You end up with hot water and peace of mind.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
