Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the tank is often leaking. That is the reason maintenance matters for Reston, VA homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in Reston, call (703) 202-2745.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

There is a short list of things you can do yourself: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

We arrive with the parts and tools the job usually needs, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Experience counts as much as equipment: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
