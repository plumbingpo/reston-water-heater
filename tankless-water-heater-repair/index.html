Tankless units are clever machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Reston, VA homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Error codes are a starting point, not a diagnosis, and we treat them that way on every Reston call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Reston Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which reveals a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so a typical tankless repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. And if your complaint is the cold-water sandwich, that brief burst of cold between two hot draws, we will tell you whether it is normal for your model or something we can reduce. Signs You Need Tankless Water Heater Repair in Reston

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Start with what is at stake. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
